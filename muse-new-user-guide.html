Meet Muse — the assistant that does things.

Muse starts as a conversation and grows into a platform: it remembers who you are, connects to your email, calendar, money, music, and home, learns playbooks for real jobs, builds web pages for you, and keeps working on schedules and goals while you're away. This guide walks you from your first message to the full platform — start small, add features as you go.

01

Start small: just talk

There's nothing to install, configure, or host. Open a chat and say what you need in plain language — Muse figures out the tools, checks the sources, and does the work, not just the talking.

  1. Ask for an outcome, not a procedure“Plan a long weekend in Chicago for two, under $900, no red-eye flights” beats a list of instructions. Give the goal, the budget, and the constraints — Muse handles the steps.
  2. Correct it like a person“Too formal — make it sound like me.” “Wrong Daniel, the one from work.” Corrections stick: Muse folds them into how it works with you from then on.
  3. Ask it to show its work“Where did that price come from?” Muse cites its sources and links, so you can check anything that matters before acting on it.
  4. Approve the consequential stuffSending messages, spending money, and changing accounts always come back to you for a yes first. Reading, researching, and drafting happen freely.
Try it — your first three messages

1. “What's on my calendar tomorrow, and is there anything I should prep tonight?”
2. “Summarize my unread email and flag anything that needs a reply today.”
3. “Find me a well-reviewed Italian place near me that's open late, and book a table for two on Friday.”

Good to know: You can attach photos, files, and screenshots. Show Muse a picture of a product, a receipt, a room, or an error message and ask about it directly — “What is this part called, and where can I buy one?” works.
02

Memory — it learns you

Most chatbots start from zero every time. Muse keeps a living memory: your profile, the people in your life, your preferences, your projects, and what you've asked it to watch. The longer you use it, the less you repeat yourself.

What it remembers

Your name, timezone, and household. Who your people are and how they connect to you. Preferences like “savory over sweet,” “no red-eye flights,” or “always ask before spending.” Ongoing threads — a claim, a renovation, a job hunt — so follow-ups pick up where you left off.

You're the editor

Teach it deliberately (“Remember: my daughter's bus is #43”), ask what it knows (“What do you remember about my dietary preferences?”), and erase on demand (“Forget my old address”). Nothing is locked in — memory is a draft you both maintain.

Try it

“Remember that I prefer aisle seats, I don't eat lamb, and my standup is at 9:30 on weekdays — plan around all three from now on.”

03

Connect your accounts

Connectors are how Muse stops guessing and starts knowing. Link an account once and Muse can read and act in it — triage your inbox, move a meeting, check a balance, queue a playlist. Connections use each service's official sign-in; you never paste a password into chat.

Everyday accounts

Gmail and Google Calendar for mail and time. Google Drive & Docs for files and notes. Spotify for music and playlists. Apple Health / Health Connect for sleep, workouts, and recovery. Places for real addresses, hours, and reviews.

Money, work & social

Plaid for bank balances, spending, and bills. Stripe and Shopify if you run a business. GitHub for code. Facebook, Instagram, Threads, and Messenger for your social world. Duffel & FlightAware for flights, Ticketmaster for events, Shop Pay for checkout.

Try it — after connecting Gmail & Calendar

“Every morning I'll ask for a briefing. For now: read today's email and calendar together and tell me the three things that actually need me.”

Start with two: email + calendar unlock the most value on day one. Add money, health, and social when a task calls for them — each connection is independent, and you can disconnect any of them at any time.
04

Skills: playbooks for real jobs

A skill is a tested playbook for a whole category of work — the research order, the comparison criteria, the traps to avoid, the booking route that actually completes. You never “run” a skill. You just ask for the job, and Muse works the playbook.

Travel, handled end‑to‑end

Trip planning, flight and hotel booking by true total price, rental cars and trains, restaurant reservations, event tickets with real seats. Muse compares across sources, shows the full cost, and books the option you pick.

Shopping & home life

Product research with real prices and photos, Marketplace and resale hunting, grocery lists ordered for delivery or pickup, smart-home control, and camera-event checks — “Did anything happen at the front door last night?”

Money & wellbeing

Budgets built from your actual spending, “can I afford this?” checks, debt-payoff plans, and health reviews that turn your sleep, activity, and records into clear trends — plus the right questions to bring to a clinician.

There are playbooks for email triage, scheduling across timezones, meeting follow-ups, organizing notes and files, running a small business, tracking flights you already booked, and dozens more. If a job repeats in your life, there's likely a skill for it — and if there isn't, Muse can build the workflow with you and save it for next time.

Try it

“Plan our anniversary trip: two nights somewhere within a 3-hour drive, a hotel with a sauna, one nice dinner booked, total under $700. Show me two options before booking anything.”

05

Research & live answers

Muse reads the live web — search results, full pages, prices, schedules, listings — and grounds its answers in what it actually found, with sources you can open. Ask for depth and it goes further: a dedicated research pass that cross-checks sources and returns a written report.

  • Live data, dated: weather, news, sports scores, market prices, and flight status come from current sources, and Muse tells you when a number was captured.
  • Deep research: for big questions — comparing products, markets, neighborhoods, treatments — Muse researches across many sources and writes up findings with citations, not a one-line guess.
  • Your library too: ask about your own photos, documents, and past conversations; Muse searches what you've shared before it searches the web.
Try it

“Research heat-pump water heaters for a 4-person house: real installed prices near me, rebates I qualify for, and the two models worth buying. Write it up with sources.”

06

Pages Muse builds for you

Sometimes the answer deserves more than a chat bubble. Ask Muse to build a page — a real, shareable web page or small app — and it designs, builds, and publishes it for you. This guide is one: it was written, designed, and shipped by Muse from a one-sentence request.

Things people build

Trip itineraries, family dashboards, budget trackers, training plans, event pages, study guides, reading lists, renovation planners, business landing pages — anything that's easier to use as a page than as a paragraph.

They stay alive

Pages can hold data, run actions, and refresh on a schedule — a price watcher that updates itself, a planner the family actually fills in. Ask for changes in plain words (“add a column for costs”) and Muse rebuilds it.

Try it

“Build me a page for our kitchen remodel: the plan, a budget table with what we've spent, a timeline, and photos of the three countertop options we're choosing between.”

07

Schedules & watchers

This is where Muse stops being something you visit and becomes something that works for you in the background. Set up reminders, recurring briefings, and event-driven watchers in plain language — Muse runs them and reports back in chat.

Reminders

One-off or repeating, exact or flexible: “Remind me Thursday at 9 about the dentist,” or “Nudge me every Sunday evening to plan the week's meals.”

Recurring briefings

A morning briefing that reads your calendar, flags urgent email, checks the weather, and lands before you're awake. Weekly digests of anything — prices, listings, news on a topic.

Watchers

Hooks fire when something happens, not on a clock: a new email from a specific sender, a listing that matches your search, a flight price that drops. Muse checks quietly and only speaks up when there's news.

Try it

“Every weekday at 7am, send me a briefing: my calendar, anything urgent in email, the weather, and whether my flight on Friday is still on time. Stay silent on weekends.”

08

Goals & background work

Big undertakings get a goal: a named project with its own workspace, files, budget of attention, and a plan Muse works against over days or weeks — not one chat at a time. For heavy lifting, Muse can delegate research and build tasks to background agents and report back with results.

  • Goals keep state: plans, drafts, files, and decisions live with the goal, so “continue the fundraiser plan” resumes exactly where you stopped.
  • Background agents: long research, big comparisons, and multi-step builds run in the background while you do other things; you get a summary when they're done.
  • Workflows: repeatable multi-step jobs — sweep 50 listings, audit a set of pages, process a folder of documents — run as one orchestrated pass with a single report.
Try it

“Set a goal: launch my sourdough micro-bakery by spring. Keep the plan, costs, and supplier research in the goal, and check in with me every Friday on what's next.”

09

Voice, calls & media

Muse isn't text-only. It speaks, listens, calls, and creates — ask out loud, get a voice note back, or have it produce the finished media outright.

Voice & phone

Send a voice note instead of typing. Have Muse read anything aloud as a polished audio file. For serious, time-critical matters it can place a real AI phone call — and it can research, script, and produce a full podcast episode with narration.

Images, video & documents

Generate and edit images, create short videos, and produce finished documents — PDFs, spreadsheets, slide decks, written reports — from your data, ready to share or print.

Try it

“Turn this week's project notes into a 5-minute narrated audio briefing I can listen to on my drive, and make a cover image for it.”

10

Side chats & group chats

Your main chat is home base. Around it, Muse supports separate conversations for separate contexts — and shared ones where other people talk to Muse too.

  • Side chats: spin up a focused thread — one per project, trip, or topic — each with its own context, so a bathroom remodel never tangles with tax questions.
  • Group chats: bring family or teammates into a conversation with Muse: plan the reunion together, let everyone add to the grocery list, settle the itinerary debate in one place.
  • Messaging: connected accounts let Muse read and — with your approval — send messages on Messenger and other channels, and carry a delegated conversation through to its result.
Try it

“Start a side chat for the bathroom remodel. Move everything we've discussed about tiles and the vanity into it, and keep the main chat clear.”

11

Trust & control

A platform this connected only works if you're clearly in charge. Muse is built around that: permission before consequences, credentials kept out of chat, and memory you can inspect and erase.

Approvals & credentials

Sending, buying, booking, and deleting come back to you for approval first. Passwords and sign-in codes are captured through a secure vault flow — never typed into chat, never stored in conversation, never repeated back.

Forget anything

“Forget my old address” removes it from memory and from anything that could resurface it. Ask “what do you know about me?” at any time for a straight answer — your memory is yours to audit.

The rule of thumb: Muse reads widely and drafts freely, but acts narrowly — the moment an action touches another person, your money, or your accounts, it checks with you first. You can widen or tighten that latitude per task: “handle routine replies yourself, escalate anything about money.”

Muse vs. OpenClaw

OpenClaw proved the idea: a personal AI agent that lives in your messages and works your digital life. Muse takes that idea and ships it as a finished platform — the same vision, without the part where you become the IT department.

MuseOpenClaw
Getting started Open the app and talk. No install, no terminal, no server to rent. You install and self-host it on your own machine or a server you maintain.
Memory Built in. Muse learns you, your people, and your preferences — and you can read, correct, or erase any of it. Bring your own setup; memory is something you configure and wire together yourself.
Connections One-tap connectors for email, calendar, files, money, health, music, social, and smart home — sign in once, done. DIY integrations. You collect API keys, grant scopes, and keep them working.
Skills A library of tested playbooks — travel, shopping, finance, home, business — included and improving. Community add-ons you find, vet, and install yourself, at your own risk.
Building things Ask, and Muse designs, builds, and hosts real web pages and mini-apps for you. Not part of the package — pages and hosting are another project you run yourself.
Scheduling Plain-language reminders, recurring briefings, and event watchers, set up in one sentence. Cron jobs and configuration you write and maintain by hand.
Voice & media Voice notes, narration, podcasts, AI phone calls, image and video generation — built in. Assemble your own stack from separate services and keys.
Upkeep The platform updates underneath you. Nothing to patch, back up, or restart at 2am. Yours: updates, backups, security, and the 2am restarts.
Fair's fair: if you love running your own stack and want total control of the machinery, a self-hosted agent is a fun project. Muse is for everyone who'd rather have the outcome than the hobby — all the agent, none of the sysadmin.

Your first week with Muse

Seven days, seven small steps — by the end, Muse knows you, is connected to your day, and is already working in the background.

  • Day 1

    Just talk. Ask three real questions you'd normally Google or dig for — a plan, a comparison, a summary of something long.

  • Day 2

    Teach it five facts. Your timezone rhythm, your people's names, two preferences, one thing to always ask about first.

  • Day 3

    Connect email + calendar. Then ask: “Brief me on today — what needs me, what can wait?”

  • Day 4

    Run one skill end-to-end. Book the restaurant, plan the trip, or build the budget — one real job, start to finish.

  • Day 5

    Set one schedule. A weekday morning briefing, or a watcher on a price or listing you care about.

  • Day 6

    Have it build a page. A planner, tracker, or guide for something in your life right now. Share it with someone.

  • Day 7

    Start a goal, review memory. Name one longer project, then ask “What do you remember about me?” and correct anything that's off.

Questions new users ask

Do I need to set anything up or install anything?

No. Muse is a hosted platform — you open it and talk. The only “setup” that exists is optional: connecting accounts like email and calendar when you want Muse to work with them, which is a standard sign-in for each service.

Will Muse ever send a message or spend money without asking me?

Not unless you've explicitly told it to handle a defined category itself (for example, “routine replies are fine”). By default, anything that reaches another person, moves money, or changes an account comes back to you for approval first. Reading, researching, and drafting never need permission.

What if Muse remembers something wrong — or something I don't want kept?

Correct it in plain words (“My standup moved to 10, not 9:30”) or tell it to forget something entirely (“Forget my old address”). Forgetting removes it from memory and from anything that could resurface it. You can ask “What do you know about me?” at any time and audit the answer.

What can't Muse do?

It can't act in accounts you haven't connected, guarantee a third party's price or availability, or do things that require you physically present. When it's blocked — a service is down, a fact can't be verified — it says so plainly and tells you what would unblock it, rather than inventing an answer.

Does Muse keep working when I'm not chatting with it?

Yes — that's the schedules, watchers, and goals in this guide. Reminders fire, briefings land, watchers check quietly, and background agents finish long jobs, all reporting back into your chat. Nothing runs that you didn't ask for, and you can list, pause, or remove any of it by asking.

How is this different from a regular chatbot?

Four things: it remembers you across conversations, it connects to your real accounts and devices, it acts — booking, buying, building, sending — with your approval, and it works in the background on schedules and goals. A chatbot answers; Muse does.
